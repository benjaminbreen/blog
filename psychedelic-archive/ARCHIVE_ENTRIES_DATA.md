## Entry 5: "Let there be Light" (1946)

**Title:** 1946: "Let there be Light"

**Slug:** 1946-let-there-be-light

**Category:** Audio & Video (documentary film)

**Era:** 1800-1950

**Date:** 1946-01-01

**Author:** John Huston (director)

**Source:** US Army Pictorial Services

**Description:** John Huston's 1946 film about recovery from wartime trauma, which was produced by the US government but never released publicly. Portrays narcosynthesis, a precursor to psychedelic-assisted therapy. The film was censored for 35 years.

**Tags:** Narcosynthesis, PTSD, 1940s, Documentary, Military, Censorship, John Huston, War trauma

**Content:**
John Huston's powerful documentary "Let There Be Light" follows the treatment of World War II veterans suffering from what we now call PTSD at Mason General Hospital on Long Island. The film showcases narcosynthesis—a treatment involving sodium amytal (sometimes called "truth serum")—as a therapeutic technique to help soldiers access repressed traumatic memories.

Commissioned by the U.S. Army Signal Corps, the film was deemed too sensitive for public viewing and was suppressed by the military for 35 years, not being released until 1981. The documentary presents an unflinching look at the psychological casualties of war and experimental treatments that would later influence psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy.
